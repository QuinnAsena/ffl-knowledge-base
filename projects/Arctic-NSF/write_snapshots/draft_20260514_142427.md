# Introduction

The ecosystems of interior Alaska are shaped by their relationship with wildfire. Climate-driven changes in the fire regime are expected to drive compositional shifts in species communities across interior Alaska. Forests on the landscape are dominated primarily by two deciduous species, trembling aspen and Alaskan birch, and two coniferous species, white and black spruce. Empirical data suggest that, with climate change induced increases in fire severity, a shift towards deciduous species dominance is likely.
